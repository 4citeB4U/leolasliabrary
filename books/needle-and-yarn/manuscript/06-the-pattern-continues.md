# Chapter Six — The Pattern Continues

Winter did not leave Milwaukee all at once.

It loosened its grip.

A little snow disappeared from the edge of the sidewalk.

Then another patch.

Icicles shortened.

Water began dripping from the roof.

One morning, the tree outside Leola's window held a tiny green bud.

Yarn spotted it first.

“Needle.”

“Yes?”

“Come here.”

“What is it?”

“Just come look.”

Needle joined her at the basket rim.

Yarn pointed.

“There.”

Needle looked.

“A bud.”

“A **green** bud.”

“Yes.”

Yarn stared at him.

“Needle, winter been outside tryin' to kill everything for months and that little thing just showed up like ain't nothin' happened.”

Needle smiled.

“That is generally how spring works.”

Yarn nudged him.

“Can't you let somethin' be magical?”

“I find it quite remarkable.”

“Remarkable ain't magical.”

“Very well.”

Needle looked solemnly toward the tiny bud.

“Magic.”

Yarn smiled.

“Thank you.”

From behind them, Scissors yawned.

“If y'all finished discussing botany, some of us was sleepin'.”

Measuring Tape stretched.

“You been awake listening the whole time.”

“That is entirely beside the point.”

Yarn looked outside again.

Spring was coming.

And somehow—

everything felt ready for it.

---

Micky's basket was still there.

Christmas had come and gone.

Then January.

February.

And now March was pushing winter aside.

What had begun as *I'll leave my crochet bag here for tomorrow* had become something else.

Micky kept coming back.

Not every day.

Life didn't work that way.

Sometimes a week passed.

Sometimes longer.

But she always returned.

And every time she did, the two baskets moved closer together.

At first, Micky sat beside Leola and watched.

Then she tried.

Then she made mistakes.

A lot of mistakes.

Yarn liked that part.

“Too tight,” she whispered one afternoon.

Needle looked at her.

“You used to do exactly that.”

“Which is how I know.”

Micky pulled the stitch.

It tightened.

Leola looked over.

“Baby, loosen your hand.”

“I am loose.”

Yarn gasped.

She looked at Needle.

“Did you hear that?”

Needle was already laughing.

“I am experiencing a rather powerful sense of familiarity.”

Micky tried again.

Too loose.

Leola shook her head.

“Now you done let the whole thing go.”

Yarn stared at Needle.

“That's exactly what you said to me!”

“I know.”

Yarn was delighted.

“Oh, I like this.”

---

Micky sighed.

“Momma, this don't look like yours.”

“It ain't supposed to.”

Micky looked over.

“What?”

Leola continued working.

“You learning.”

“I know, but look at this.”

“I see it.”

“It look terrible.”

Leola put her own work down.

She took Micky's piece.

Looked at it.

Turned it.

There were uneven stitches.

A crooked edge.

One section had somehow grown wider than the rest.

Leola smiled.

“Looks like somebody learning to crochet.”

Micky laughed.

“Momma.”

“What you want me to tell you?”

“That it's good.”

“No.”

Micky stared.

Leola smiled.

“It ain't good yet.”

Micky burst out laughing.

“See?”

“But that's the *yet* part.”

Leola handed it back.

“You keep worrying about making something pretty.”

She pointed toward Micky's hands.

“Right now, we're teaching these.”

Micky looked down.

“My hands?”

“Your hands.”

Leola picked up her own work.

“Your mind can understand a stitch before your fingers do.”

Needle looked toward Yarn.

Yarn whispered:

“She sound like Old Spool.”

From the basket:

“I heard that.”

Yarn sighed.

“Why do I keep forgetting?”

---

Leola demonstrated again.

Slowly.

“Don't fight the yarn.”

Micky watched.

“Let it move.”

Hook through.

Yarn over.

Pull through.

Again.

“See?”

Micky nodded.

Then tried.

Wrong.

She sighed.

Again.

Better.

Again.

Better still.

Leola smiled.

“There.”

Micky looked.

“Oh.”

“You felt that one, didn't you?”

“Yeah.”

“That's what you looking for.”

Micky tried another.

The movement wasn't perfect.

But it had rhythm.

Leola nodded.

“Again.”

And they continued.

Not dramatically.

No grand breakthrough.

No music.

No applause.

Just—

again.

Again.

Again.

Yarn watched.

She understood now.

Mastery didn't always arrive like lightning.

Sometimes it arrived so quietly you didn't notice it until your hands could do today what they couldn't do yesterday.

---

After Micky left that evening, the baskets came alive.

The younger Needle climbed out.

“I think she getting better.”

Needle nodded.

“She is.”

Micky's little Scissors hopped up.

“She still cuttin' tails too long.”

Our Scissors opened his blades.

“Better too long than too short.”

The little Scissors looked at him.

“That's what I said!”

Scissors turned proudly toward Measuring Tape.

“You hear that?”

“Yes.”

“Wisdom.”

“One correct statement in four months.”

“Still counts.”

Marker had been practicing with the younger stitch markers.

He stood on top of a button.

“I have an announcement.”

Nobody looked.

“I SAID I HAVE AN ANNOUNCEMENT.”

Hook sighed.

“What?”

Marker puffed himself up.

“I have not been lost in nine days.”

The smaller markers applauded.

Scissors whispered:

“He was lost Tuesday.”

Marker spun around.

“I WAS NOT LOST.”

“You was under the couch.”

“I knew where I was.”

“We didn't.”

“That's y'all's problem.”

The baskets erupted.

Yarn leaned against Needle.

Their family had gotten louder.

Somehow—

Yarn liked that too.

---

The weeks passed.

Micky learned chains.

Then rows.

Then how to recognize when she'd added a stitch.

Then how to recognize when she'd dropped one.

One afternoon, she caught her own mistake before Leola said anything.

“Wait.”

Leola looked up.

“What?”

Micky studied her work.

“I did something wrong back here.”

Leola didn't help.

“Did you?”

Micky counted.

Then counted again.

“There.”

She pointed.

“I added one.”

Leola smiled.

“So what you gon' do?”

Micky looked at her.

“Take it back.”

“Then take it back.”

Micky carefully unraveled several stitches.

Yarn watched.

No panic.

No frustration.

Just correction.

Then Micky worked the row again.

This time—

right.

Leola didn't clap.

Didn't make a big fuss.

She simply smiled.

“Mmm-hmm.”

Micky looked up.

“What?”

“Nothin'.”

“You got that look.”

“What look?”

“That Momma look.”

Leola laughed.

“I don't know what you talkin' about.”

“Yes, you do.”

Micky looked down at her work.

Then smiled too.

Yarn whispered:

“She proud.”

Needle nodded.

“Very.”

---

Spring moved closer.

The old snow disappeared completely.

Birds returned.

Windows opened.

Fresh air came through the house.

The heavy winter projects went away.

Lighter yarn appeared.

Cotton.

Pastels.

Soft greens.

Yellow.

Blue.

Yarn looked around.

“Everybody changing clothes.”

Needle smiled.

“They are fibers.”

“You know what I mean.”

The world was beginning again.

But it wasn't beginning from nothing.

That mattered.

The tree outside wasn't a different tree.

Needle wasn't a different Needle.

Yarn wasn't a different Yarn.

Leola wasn't starting over.

Neither was Micky.

Spring didn't erase what winter had done.

It grew from it.

---

One Wednesday afternoon, Micky finished her first complete piece.

A small square.

Nothing fancy.

Not a sweater.

Not a blanket.

Not something anybody would put behind glass.

A square.

Micky held it up.

Leola inspected it.

One edge was slightly crooked.

The tension changed halfway through.

A loose tail hung from one corner.

Micky smiled.

“I made this.”

Leola nodded.

“You surely did.”

Micky turned it over.

“I know it ain't much.”

Leola's expression changed.

“Don't do that.”

“Do what?”

“Don't talk down about your own work.”

Micky looked at her.

“You ain't gotta pretend it's perfect.”

“I know it ain't perfect.”

Leola took the square.

“But you couldn't make this when we started.”

Micky became quiet.

Leola handed it back.

“So don't look at what it ain't.”

She touched Micky's hand.

“Look at what you learned to do.”

Yarn stared.

Needle glanced toward her.

She was emotional.

“Yarn?”

“I'm fine.”

“You are crying.”

“I said I'm fine.”

Scissors whispered:

“She got allergies.”

Yarn laughed through it.

“Shut up.”

---

Micky looked at the square again.

“You know what?”

“What?” Leola asked.

“I wanna keep going.”

Leola smiled.

“Then keep going.”

“No, I mean really.”

Micky sat forward.

“I wanna get better.”

“Good.”

“I wanna learn the patterns. The different stitches. I wanna learn how you do all that stuff.”

Leola raised an eyebrow.

“All that stuff?”

“You know what I mean.”

“I do.”

Micky laughed.

“What if I come over regular?”

Leola looked at her.

“You already over here regular.”

“No, Momma. I mean we set it.”

Leola listened.

“Like training.”

“Training?”

“Yes.”

Micky thought.

“What about the next couple weeks?”

Leola smiled.

“What days?”

They worked it out.

A morning.

Then another.

Time set aside.

Not whenever they happened to get around to it.

Intentional time.

Mother and daughter.

Practice.

Mistakes.

Correction.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Micky pointed toward her basket.

“I'll just leave my stuff here.”

Leola nodded.

“Leave it.”

“Then I ain't got no excuse.”

“You always got excuses.”

“Momma.”

Leola laughed.

“I'll be ready.”

Micky smiled.

“So will I.”

---

After she left, both baskets erupted.

“TRAINING!”

Marker bounced.

“We training!”

“You ain't training nobody,” Hook said.

“I can train.”

“To do what?”

Marker thought.

“Mark.”

Scissors laughed.

The younger Needle climbed beside Needle.

“You think we get to work together?”

“Probably.”

“Think she'll use me?”

“Yes.”

“How you know?”

Needle smiled.

“Because you're hers.”

The younger Needle remembered that.

His posture lifted.

Yarn watched Needle.

He'd become different with the younger tools.

Patient.

Less certain that every lesson needed a correction.

More willing to let somebody discover.

She liked this version of him.

Then she realized—

it wasn't a different version.

It was simply more of him.

---

That night, both baskets stayed awake longer than usual.

Spring air came through the cracked window.

Everybody talked about the past year.

Marker remembered Yarn arriving.

“I wasn't scared of you.”

“You hid behind Measuring Tape.”

“I was observing.”

Scissors laughed.

“Sound like Needle.”

Needle looked offended.

Yarn remembered her first stitch.

Hook remembered the scarf.

Measuring Tape remembered the first time Yarn got jealous over brown yarn.

“I WAS NOT JEALOUS.”

The room erupted.

Brown Yarn shouted:

“You absolutely were!”

Yarn gasped.

“You stay outta this!”

Even Needle laughed.

Then somebody mentioned the break.

The laughter softened.

Not disappeared.

Just softened.

Needle looked at his repaired tip.

Yarn leaned against him.

Scissors said quietly:

“That was a bad day.”

“Yes,” Needle said.

Marker looked toward him.

“But you came back.”

Needle smiled.

“Yes.”

“I did.”

Then Purple Yarn began talking about Christmas.

Naturally, his version had become considerably more dramatic.

“There were at least eighty people.”

“There were not.”

“Seventy.”

“No.”

“Sixty?”

“Purple.”

“Large gathering.”

“Better.”

They laughed.

Stories had already started becoming memories.

Memories becoming family history.

---

The evening before Micky's first scheduled training morning, nobody could sleep.

Not Leola's basket.

Not Micky's.

The younger tools were nervous.

The older tools were giving advice nobody had requested.

Scissors had declared himself “Assistant Instructor.”

Nobody had approved that appointment.

Measuring Tape was organizing.

Marker was reorganizing what Measuring Tape had organized.

Hook was undoing Marker.

Yarn watched the chaos.

“Needle.”

“Yes?”

“You think Miss Leola's family act like this before something important?”

Needle looked around.

“I strongly suspect so.”

Yarn smiled.

“Good.”

Outside, the night was beginning to thin.

Not morning yet.

But close.

That deep blue hour when darkness had started giving up, even though the sun hadn't arrived.

The basket conversations grew quieter.

Everyone was tired.

Excited.

Remembering.

Wondering.

Then—

footsteps.

Everybody froze.

Leola entered.

Already awake.

Yarn looked toward Needle.

Leola was dressed comfortably.

Not for company yet.

Not quite.

She carried a fresh cup of coffee.

Steam curled above it.

Leola set the cup down.

Then reached into the basket.

Her hand found Needle.

The other found Yarn.

Yarn looked down.

Everyone was watching.

Scissors.

Measuring Tape.

Marker.

Hook.

Old Spool.

Purple.

Brown.

The younger Needle.

Micky's Scissors.

Both little families.

Yarn wanted to say something.

Couldn't.

Leola was watching.

The hands lifted them.

And carried them away.

---

The front door opened.

Cool spring air rushed in.

Leola stepped onto the porch.

Yarn had never been outside the house like this.

Not since the shop.

The world smelled different.

Wet earth.

Grass.

Morning.

Somewhere nearby, a bird tried the first few notes of a song.

Leola walked to the porch bench.

She laid Needle and Yarn together on it.

Then sat in her rocking chair.

Coffee in hand.

And waited.

Yarn didn't move at first.

Neither did Needle.

They weren't sure.

Leola could see them.

But she wasn't looking directly at them.

She was looking toward the horizon.

Needle whispered:

“Yarn.”

“Yes?”

“Look.”

She did.

The sky was changing.

Dark blue gave way to violet.

Then pink.

Then a thin line of gold appeared between the houses.

Yarn stared.

“Oh, my stars.”

Needle smiled.

She had said the same thing the day Leola chose her.

Neither mentioned it.

Leola rocked.

*Cre-eak.*

Forward.

Back.

*Cre-eak.*

Forward.

Back.

She lifted her coffee.

Steam moved through the cold morning air.

The first edge of the sun appeared.

Yarn moved closer to Needle.

“You ever seen this?”

“Many times.”

“Like this?”

Needle looked at her.

“No.”

“Me neither.”

The sun rose a little more.

Golden light touched Needle's repaired tip.

Then Yarn's sunset-orange fibers.

For a moment, they almost glowed.

---

Inside the house, the basket family watched from the window as best they could.

Marker had climbed onto Measuring Tape.

Scissors stood on Hook.

Purple complained that somebody was blocking his view.

Old Spool didn't climb at all.

He didn't need to.

He seemed to know what was happening.

The younger Needle whispered:

“Why she take just them?”

Old Spool smiled.

“Some moments belong to everybody.”

He looked toward the porch.

“And some belong to the ones who made the journey.”

---

Yarn watched the sunrise.

“So this spring?”

Needle smiled.

“Yes.”

“The beginning?”

“In a manner of speaking.”

Yarn considered that.

“But we already had a beginning.”

“We did.”

“So how we get another one?”

Needle looked toward the sun.

“I suppose beginnings aren't something you only get once.”

Yarn liked that.

A lot.

Behind them, Leola rocked.

The woman who had chosen Yarn.

The woman whose hands had brought Needle and Yarn together.

The woman whose fear had once traveled through those same hands.

The woman who had carried Needle away broken.

The woman who had brought him home.

The woman who had spent a lifetime turning thread into warmth.

Today, those hands would begin teaching her daughter how to do the same.

Yarn looked toward Leola.

“You think she know?”

“What?”

“What she passin' down.”

Needle considered.

“Yes.”

“All of it?”

Needle looked toward the house.

Toward Micky's basket.

Toward the tools waiting.

Toward a future neither of them could see.

“No.”

Yarn smiled.

“Good.”

“Why?”

“Means there's still surprises.”

Needle laughed softly.

---

A car turned onto the street.

Yarn heard it.

Needle did too.

It slowed.

Micky.

Training morning.

Leola heard the car.

She smiled but didn't immediately stand.

She took one more sip of coffee.

One more moment.

The sun cleared the horizon.

Morning arrived.

Yarn leaned against Needle.

“Needle?”

“Yes?”

“You remember the shop?”

“Yes.”

“I thought gettin' chosen was the end of the story.”

Needle smiled.

“And?”

“Turns out that was just page one.”

Needle looked at her.

Yarn continued.

“We made things.”

“Yes.”

“Messed up things.”

“Several.”

“Broke things.”

Needle glanced at his tip.

“Yes.”

“Fixed things.”

“Yes.”

“Loved people.”

“Yes.”

“Loved each other.”

Needle smiled.

“Very much.”

Yarn looked toward the front door as the sound of Micky's car stopped.

“And now somebody else about to start.”

Needle looked toward Leola.

“Yes.”

---

The car door closed.

Footsteps approached.

Leola stood.

She picked up Needle and Yarn.

Held them together in one hand.

Not tightly.

Not loosely.

Just enough.

The front gate opened.

“Morning, Momma!”

Leola smiled.

“Mornin', baby.”

“You ready for me?”

Leola looked down briefly at Needle and Yarn.

Then toward her daughter.

“Been ready.”

Micky laughed.

“Got coffee?”

“In the kitchen.”

“I knew I came to the right place.”

They walked inside together.

Mother.

Daughter.

Teacher.

Learner.

Behind them, the spring sun continued rising.

---

Leola placed Needle and Yarn back into the basket.

The instant she turned away—

everybody erupted.

“How was it?”

“What happened?”

“Did you see the sun?”

“Was it cold?”

“Why'd she take y'all?”

Marker bounced.

“DID YOU GO OUTSIDE?”

Yarn laughed.

“Everybody hush!”

Nobody hushed.

Of course they didn't.

Needle looked at Yarn.

Yarn looked at Needle.

And neither tried very hard to quiet them.

Across the room, Micky opened her crochet bag.

Leola pulled her chair closer.

Two baskets sat beside two chairs.

Micky picked up her smaller Needle.

Leola reached toward Yarn.

The younger Needle looked terrified.

Needle called softly from the basket:

“You know what to do.”

The younger Needle looked toward him.

Needle smiled.

“Start.”

Micky positioned her hands.

Wrong.

Leola laughed.

“Hold on, baby.”

She reached over.

Adjusted Micky's fingers.

“Not so tight.”

Micky sighed.

“I ain't tight.”

From inside the basket, Yarn's eyes widened.

She slowly turned toward Needle.

Needle was already looking at her.

Neither lasted.

They burst out laughing.

Scissors nearly fell over.

“I HEARD THAT BEFORE!”

Measuring Tape wrapped around him.

“Would you hush?”

Micky couldn't hear them.

She looked at Leola.

“What?”

Leola was smiling too.

“Nothin', baby.”

“You laughing at me.”

“I ain't laughing at you.”

“Yes, you are.”

Leola shook her head.

“I'm remembering something.”

“What?”

Leola looked down at the yarn in Micky's hands.

For a moment, her expression softened.

“Just remembering what it felt like when my own hands was learning.”

Micky looked at her mother.

Then at her hands.

“Well, these hands got a long way to go.”

Leola smiled.

“Then we better get started.”

---

Micky repositioned the Needle.

Leola watched.

“That's better.”

“Like this?”

“Mmm-hmm.”

“Now what?”

“Yarn over.”

Micky tried.

The yarn slipped.

“Oh, come on.”

“Again.”

She tried.

Missed.

“Again.”

Tried.

Caught it.

“There.”

Micky smiled.

“I got it.”

“One time.”

“Momma.”

Leola laughed.

“Do it again.”

Micky did.

A little smoother.

Again.

Again.

And something familiar filled the room.

**Over.**

**Catch.**

**Through.**

**Again.**

Yarn became very still.

Needle looked at her.

“You hear it?”

“Mmm-hmm.”

It was their rhythm.

But it wasn't.

These were different hands.

A different Needle.

A different strand.

A different beginning.

Still—

**over.**

**Catch.**

**Through.**

**Again.**

Yarn smiled.

“Pattern continues.”

Old Spool's voice came softly from below.

“Always does.”

---

Micky made several stitches.

Then frowned.

“Momma.”

“What?”

“This one don't look right.”

Leola leaned closer.

“Nope.”

“What'd I do?”

“You tell me.”

“I don't know.”

“Look.”

Micky studied it.

Leola waited.

Didn't rescue her.

Didn't take the work away.

Didn't fix it for her.

Micky turned the stitch.

Looked again.

Then—

“Oh.”

“What?”

“I went through the wrong loop.”

Leola smiled.

“So what you gon' do?”

Micky sighed.

“Take it back.”

“Take it back.”

Micky carefully pulled the stitch out.

No shame.

No frustration.

Just information.

She repositioned the Needle.

Tried again.

The stitch formed correctly.

“There you go,” Leola said.

Micky smiled.

Inside the basket, Yarn whispered:

“She saw it herself.”

Needle nodded.

“Yes.”

“Miss Leola could've just told her.”

“She could have.”

“But she didn't.”

“No.”

Yarn watched them.

“Why?”

Needle smiled.

“Because knowing the answer isn't the same thing as learning how to find it.”

Yarn looked at him.

“Well, listen to you.”

Needle straightened slightly.

“I have accumulated some experience.”

Scissors groaned.

“He gon' be impossible now.”

---

Morning moved forward.

Sunlight climbed across the floor.

Leola's coffee cooled.

Micky's little practice row grew.

It wasn't straight.

Not quite.

The tension wandered.

Some stitches sat proudly beside each other.

Others looked like they'd had an argument.

But the row existed.

Micky held it up.

“Look.”

Leola looked.

Micky smiled.

“Better than last time.”

“Sure is.”

Micky turned it.

“You think I can really learn this?”

Leola didn't answer immediately.

She looked at her daughter.

Not at the stitches.

At her.

“Do you wanna learn?”

“Yes.”

“Then yes.”

“That simple?”

“No.”

Micky laughed.

Leola did too.

“Simple ain't the same as easy.”

Micky nodded.

Leola pointed toward the row.

“You gon' get frustrated.”

“I already am.”

“You gon' make things crooked.”

“Already did that too.”

“You gon' pull out work you spent an hour makin'.”

Micky grimaced.

“I don't like that one.”

“You gon' do it anyway.”

Micky laughed.

Leola continued.

“Then one day your hands gon' do something you been fighting with for weeks…”

She smiled.

“…and you ain't even gon' notice at first.”

Micky looked down at her hands.

“And that's when I know?”

“Know what?”

“That I learned it.”

Leola shook her head.

“No.”

Micky looked confused.

“That's when you know you're **learning**.”

Leola picked up her own work.

“You ain't never finished learning a craft.”

Micky smiled.

“Not even you?”

“Especially not me.”

---

The basket was quiet.

Even Scissors.

Yarn looked around at everybody.

The older tools.

The newer ones.

The remnants of projects.

The repaired Needle beside her.

The younger Needle moving through Micky's practice row.

She understood something.

All year, she'd thought the important thing was what they were making.

The scarf.

The hat.

The gifts.

The stitches.

But maybe those were only part of it.

Something else had been made too.

Micky's hands were changing.

Just as Yarn's had.

Just as Needle's had after his repair.

Just as Leola's must have changed long before any of them could remember.

Skills lived in hands.

But they didn't begin there.

Somebody showed those hands what to do.

---

When the lesson ended, Micky didn't pack up.

She left the practice piece on the table.

“I'll finish that Wednesday.”

Leola raised an eyebrow.

“You coming Wednesday?”

“We scheduled Wednesday.”

“You remember?”

Micky looked offended.

“Momma.”

Leola laughed.

“I'm just asking.”

“I'll be here.”

“And Saturday?”

“Saturday too.”

“For the next two weeks?”

Micky smiled.

“For the next two weeks.”

Leola held out her hand.

Micky looked at it.

“What?”

“Agreement.”

Micky laughed and shook it.

“Agreement.”

Then Leola pulled her daughter into a hug.

Micky laughed against her shoulder.

“Momma!”

“Come here.”

They held each other.

Not because anybody was leaving forever.

Not because something terrible had happened.

Not because Christmas demanded a family hug.

Just because sometimes love didn't need an occasion.

Yarn watched.

Needle moved beside her.

“She'll teach somebody someday,” Yarn whispered.

“Micky?”

“Mmm-hmm.”

“Perhaps.”

Yarn looked at him.

“There go that word.”

Needle smiled.

“All right.”

He watched mother and daughter.

“Yes.”

“She will.”

---

Micky eventually left.

But her basket stayed.

Just as promised.

Two baskets remained beside Leola's chair.

The house quieted.

Spring sunlight filled the room.

Marker climbed onto the rim.

“So what now?”

Scissors stretched.

“What you mean, what now?”

“The story.”

Everybody looked at him.

“What story?” Hook asked.

“Ours.”

Marker looked around.

“We had winter.”

“Yes.”

“Christmas.”

“Yes.”

“Needle broke.”

Needle cleared his throat.

“We remember.”

“Yarn came from the store.”

“Yes.”

“Miss Micky learning now.”

“Yes.”

Marker frowned.

“So are we done?”

The question quieted the basket.

Yarn looked at Needle.

Needle looked toward Old Spool.

Old Spool chuckled.

“Why everybody looking at me?”

“Because you old,” Scissors said.

Measuring Tape slapped him.

“What?”

Old Spool laughed.

Then looked at Marker.

“No, child.”

“We ain't done?”

“No.”

“When we done?”

Old Spool thought about it.

“I reckon that's the wrong question.”

Marker tilted.

“What the right one?”

Old Spool looked toward Micky's unfinished practice row.

“What's next?”

Marker considered that.

“Oh.”

Then he smiled.

“I like that question better.”

“So do I.”

---

That afternoon, Leola sat alone in her chair.

She picked up the little square Micky had made weeks earlier.

The first one.

Crooked edge.

Changing tension.

Loose tail.

Leola kept it.

Yarn smiled when she saw it.

“She saved it.”

Needle nodded.

“Of course.”

Leola placed the square into a small wooden box.

Inside were other things.

Old swatches.

Bits of work.

Little pieces from years before.

Some beautiful.

Some not.

All kept.

Leola closed the box.

Yarn whispered:

“Why keep the bad ones?”

Needle looked toward the box.

“Maybe because they aren't bad.”

Yarn waited.

“They're beginnings.”

She smiled.

Outside, another bud opened on the tree.

---

That evening, the house settled early.

Nobody felt like making a lot of noise.

Not sad.

Just peaceful.

The kind of quiet that comes after something has found where it belongs.

Yarn and Needle sat together near the basket rim.

The window was open.

Warm air moved through.

“You know what I remember?” Yarn asked.

“What?”

“The first day.”

“So do I.”

“You barely talked.”

Needle looked offended.

“I was courteous.”

“You said 'Afternoon.'”

“A perfectly acceptable greeting.”

“I thought you was stuck-up.”

“I thought you were exhausting.”

Yarn gasped.

“You did not.”

“I absolutely did.”

“And now?”

Needle looked at her.

“Now I know you are exhausting.”

Yarn smacked him with a strand.

Needle laughed.

She did too.

Then she settled beside him.

“And?”

“And what?”

“You know what.”

Needle smiled.

“And I cannot imagine this basket without you.”

Yarn became quiet.

Needle nudged her.

“That was the part where you say something.”

“I know.”

“You have never had difficulty before.”

“Shut up.”

Needle laughed.

Yarn looked toward him.

“I can't imagine me without this basket.”

She looked around.

“Without Scissors.”

From below:

“I heard that.”

“Without Measuring Tape.”

“Mmm-hmm.”

“Marker.”

“I'm right here!”

“Hook.”

Hook smiled.

“Old Spool.”

No answer.

They looked.

Old Spool was asleep.

Scissors whispered:

“That's cold.”

Yarn laughed.

“And you.”

Needle moved closer.

“Especially you.”

---

The sun lowered.

The room became gold.

Then amber.

Then blue.

Nobody rushed the evening.

There was no emergency coming.

No dramatic goodbye.

No problem waiting to be solved before the chapter could end.

Tomorrow would come.

Then Wednesday.

Then Saturday.

Micky would sit beside Leola.

She would get stitches wrong.

Then right.

Then wrong again.

She would learn.

Needle and Yarn would make more things.

Scissors would tell bad jokes.

Measuring Tape would correct him.

Marker would get lost and insist he wasn't.

New yarn would arrive.

Old yarn would leave as finished work.

Children would grow.

Great-grandchildren would become grandchildren with children of their own someday.

And somewhere in all of that—

the pattern would continue.

---

Yarn looked toward the porch.

“Needle?”

“Yes?”

“I wanna see another sunrise.”

Needle smiled.

“I suspect there will be one tomorrow.”

“You know what I mean.”

“Yes.”

He did.

Yarn leaned against him.

“Maybe Miss Leola take us out again.”

“Maybe.”

“Perhaps,” Yarn teased.

Needle groaned.

“Don't start.”

She laughed.

Outside, the last light disappeared.

Inside, two baskets rested beside one another.

One belonging to a mother.

One belonging to her daughter.

Neither basket knew exactly what would be made next.

That was all right.

Because a pattern didn't have to end just because one part of it was complete.

Sometimes the most beautiful ending was simply reaching the place where somebody else's beginning could start.

And somewhere inside the old wicker basket, Yarn finally understood what Old Spool had meant all those months ago.

You didn't stop being what you were when you became something more.

She was still the sunset-orange Yarn from the bottom of the shop basket.

Needle was still the serious silver fellow who had barely said hello.

But now—

they were also every stitch they'd made.

Every mistake they'd corrected.

Every person they'd warmed.

Every frightening moment they'd survived.

Every laugh.

Every argument.

Every quiet night.

Every lesson.

Every piece of love they'd helped Leola put into the world.

And tomorrow—

there would be more.

Yarn closed her eyes.

Needle rested beside her.

Two baskets sat together.

And somewhere beneath the spring moon, another generation of hands was already beginning to remember the rhythm.

**Over.**

**Catch.**

**Through.**

**Again.**

**Thread by thread.**

**Stitch by stitch.**

**Generation by generation.**

**Together.**

### — End of Chapter Six —
