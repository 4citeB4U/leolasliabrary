# Chapter Three — Working Together

Rain had been tapping against Leola's window since before daylight.

Not hard.

Just steady.

*Tap.*

*Tap-tap.*

*Tap.*

The kind of rain that made a house feel warmer simply because you were inside it.

In the wicker basket beside Leola's chair, Yarn was awake.

Needle wasn't.

Or at least he was pretending not to be.

Yarn stared at him.

Then at the ceiling.

Then back at him.

She rolled a little closer.

Nothing.

Closer.

Nothing.

Just a little—

“Yarn.”

She stopped.

“Yes?”

“You are approximately one inch from my face.”

“Oh.”

She rolled backward.

A little.

“Better?”

“No.”

She rolled another half inch.

“How 'bout now?”

Needle opened one eye.

“You have an entire basket.”

“I got cold.”

“You are yarn.”

“That don't mean I can't be cold.”

Needle stared at her.

Yarn stared back.

From underneath them came Scissors' voice.

“Would y'all either fall in love or fall asleep? Some of us is tryin' to rest.”

Yarn nearly unraveled.

“SCISSORS!”

Needle sat straight up.

“We are doing no such thing.”

“Mmm-hmm.”

Measuring Tape whispered:

“Leave them babies alone.”

“I didn't bother nobody.”

“You announced their business to the whole basket.”

“Ain't no business to announce.”

Needle nodded firmly.

“Precisely.”

Yarn looked at him.

“Well, you ain't gotta agree *that* fast.”

Needle blinked.

“I—”

Scissors started laughing.

Measuring Tape groaned.

“Lord, give me strength.”

---

Footsteps.

Instant silence.

Leola entered carrying her coffee.

The rain followed her in the sound of little drops against the window.

She set the cup beside her chair.

Sat.

And reached into the basket.

Yarn felt those familiar fingers close around her.

Needle rose in the other hand.

They met above the basket.

Yarn whispered:

“Mornin'.”

“Good morning.”

“You sleep good?”

“I did until somebody invaded my personal space.”

“I told you I was cold.”

“You are still yarn.”

Yarn frowned.

“You keep bringin' that up like it's relevant.”

The hands positioned them.

Needle turned.

Yarn crossed.

And their morning began.

**Over.**

**Catch.**

**Through.**

**Again.**

---

Something had changed.

Neither of them said it.

But they both felt it.

Leola's hands didn't have to correct them nearly as much anymore.

Needle knew when Yarn was coming.

Yarn knew where Needle needed her.

When he turned, she loosened.

When she crossed, he caught her.

When a loop resisted, neither pulled harder.

They adjusted.

Together.

The rhythm had become almost effortless.

Almost.

“Too tight.”

“There you go again.”

“It is.”

“I know what I'm doin'.”

Needle stopped.

“You have been crocheting for two days.”

“And?”

“And yesterday you attempted to invent a stitch.”

“It could've worked.”

“It formed a knot.”

“A creative knot.”

“It took Miss Leola three minutes to undo it.”

Yarn lowered her voice.

“She ain't gotta know we callin' it a knot.”

Needle laughed.

The hands continued.

The fabric beneath them had grown considerably.

Yarn looked down.

“Needle.”

“Yes?”

“I think it's long.”

“It is.”

“How long?”

From the basket came Measuring Tape's muffled voice.

“FINALLY SOMEBODY NEED ME.”

Yarn burst out laughing.

Leola's hand paused.

Needle whispered:

“You're going to get us caught.”

“She can't hear me.”

“You don't know that.”

Yarn became very still.

She looked toward Leola.

Needle waited.

Yarn whispered:

“You think she can?”

Needle looked at the hands holding them.

“I've wondered.”

That silenced Yarn.

For about four seconds.

“Ask her.”

“I am not asking Miss Leola whether she can hear her yarn talking.”

“Why?”

“What precisely would you like me to say?”

Yarn lowered her voice into a serious imitation of him.

“Pardon me, Miss Leola, but might I inquire whether you've been listenin' to our private conversations?”

Needle stared at her.

Yarn grinned.

“That's not what I sound like.”

“Sounded just like you.”

“It did not.”

The hands began moving again.

Yarn laughed.

And Needle—

despite himself—

laughed with her.

---

Later that morning, Leola stopped.

She lifted the fabric.

Turned it lengthwise.

Yarn tried to see.

“Needle.”

“Yes?”

“What are we makin'?”

“I still don't know.”

“It's long.”

“Observant.”

“And narrow.”

“Also observant.”

Yarn gasped.

“A scarf.”

Needle looked again.

“It may be.”

“A scarf!”

The idea thrilled her.

“We makin' a scarf!”

“Possibly.”

“Needle, have some joy.”

“I have joy.”

“Where?”

Needle ignored her.

Yarn looked at the growing fabric.

A scarf.

A real thing.

Something somebody could wear.

Then she noticed something.

“Needle?”

“Yes?”

“If that's a scarf...”

She hesitated.

“Then that's me.”

Needle looked down.

Much of the original skein was gone now.

Not gone exactly.

Changed.

Row after row of sunset-orange Yarn had become fabric.

She looked at herself.

Then at the scarf.

Then herself again.

Her excitement softened.

“Oh.”

Needle understood.

“You're thinking about what Old Spool said.”

Yarn nodded.

“I know he said I don't stop bein' me.”

“No.”

“But look how much of me ain't in the basket no more.”

Needle was quiet.

“What happens when all of me is over there?”

Needle didn't have an answer.

For the first time since they'd met, Yarn wasn't joking.

And for the first time, Needle couldn't fix the problem by explaining the proper way to make a stitch.

He looked toward the growing scarf.

Then back at what remained of Yarn.

“I don't know.”

She looked at him.

Needle hated saying those words.

Yarn knew that.

“You don't?”

“No.”

The hands rested.

Rain whispered against the window.

Needle moved as close to Yarn as Leola's fingers allowed.

“But wherever you end up…”

Yarn waited.

Needle continued.

“…I'll know where every stitch came from.”

Her fibers went very still.

“You would?”

“I was there.”

Yarn looked down at the scarf.

“So if I become that…”

“Yes.”

“…you'd still know me?”

Needle answered without hesitation.

“I would know you anywhere.”

Yarn looked at him.

And something happened between them that neither had words for yet.

The rain kept falling.

The clock kept ticking.

Leola lifted her coffee.

But for Yarn, the whole room seemed to become very quiet.

Needle finally looked away.

“We should continue.”

Yarn's voice came softer than usual.

“Yeah.”

The hands returned.

**Over.**

**Catch.**

**Through.**

**Again.**

Only now the rhythm felt different.

---

By afternoon, the rain had become heavier.

The room darkened enough that Leola switched on the lamp beside her chair.

Golden light fell across her hands.

The scarf had grown.

Yarn had become quieter.

Needle noticed.

“You all right?”

“Mmm-hmm.”

“That is not an answer.”

Yarn looked at him.

“You been around Measuring Tape too long.”

“She has certain useful qualities.”

“Don't ever tell her that.”

“I have no intention of doing so.”

Yarn smiled.

Then the smile faded.

“What if whoever gets me don't like me?”

Needle stopped.

“What?”

“The scarf.”

“They will.”

“You don't know that.”

“No.”

“What if they think the color too bright?”

“They're wrong.”

Yarn looked surprised.

Needle continued.

“What?”

“Nothin'.”

“What?”

“You didn't even think about that.”

“There was nothing to think about.”

Yarn smiled.

“So you like my color?”

Needle realized too late what he'd done.

“I didn't say—”

“You like my color.”

“I said anyone who disliked it would be incorrect.”

“That's the same thing.”

“It is not.”

“Needle likes my color.”

“Yarn.”

“Needle thinks I'm pretty.”

“I said no such thing.”

Scissors' voice drifted faintly from the basket.

“HE DO.”

Needle closed his eye.

Yarn laughed so hard Leola had to adjust her tension.

---

But then—

something went wrong.

Needle entered the next stitch.

Yarn crossed.

The hook of the movement caught.

Too tight.

Needle pulled.

Yarn resisted.

“Wait.”

“I have it.”

“No, you don't.”

Needle pulled again.

The loop tightened.

“Needle.”

“I can fix it.”

“Stop pullin'!”

The stitch cinched down.

Needle strained.

Yarn felt herself twist.

“YOU HURTIN' ME!”

Needle froze.

Leola's hands froze too.

Silence.

Needle released the pressure immediately.

Yarn loosened.

The loop remained ugly and tight.

Neither spoke.

Leola examined it.

Then her fingers did something Yarn hadn't experienced before.

They began undoing the stitch.

Yarn felt herself being pulled backward.

“No!”

Needle watched.

The previous stitch came undone too.

“She's tearin' it up!”

“No. Wait.”

Another stitch disappeared.

Yarn panicked.

“Needle, she's undoing us!”

Needle looked carefully at Leola's hands.

“No.”

“How you gon' say no? I can see it!”

“Yarn, look.”

The hands stopped.

Three stitches had been removed.

The mistake was gone.

Everything before it remained.

Then Leola repositioned them.

Yarn stared.

“She only took out the bad part.”

Needle nodded.

The hands began again.

Slowly.

Needle entered.

Yarn crossed.

Neither rushed.

**Over.**

**Catch.**

**Through.**

The new stitch settled into place.

Clean.

Even.

Yarn stared.

Needle whispered:

“Better.”

Yarn looked at him.

“You could've just listened to me.”

Needle's satisfaction disappeared.

He knew she was right.

“I could have.”

“You thought you had to fix it.”

“Yes.”

“By yourself.”

Needle looked down.

“Yes.”

Yarn waited for an excuse.

None came.

Needle said:

“I'm sorry.”

Yarn softened.

“You scared me.”

“I know.”

“And you hurt.”

Needle looked up.

“I know.”

She moved slightly closer.

“Next time I say stop…”

“I stop.”

No argument.

No explanation.

Yarn nodded.

“All right.”

The hands moved again.

Their rhythm returned.

Not quite the same rhythm.

Something better.

Because this one contained something the old rhythm hadn't needed yet.

**Forgiveness.**

---

That evening, the rain finally stopped.

Leola finished the last row.

The hands paused.

Needle felt it first.

Yarn did too.

Something was different.

Leola reached for Scissors.

Inside the basket—

Scissors' eyes widened.

“Oh.”

Measuring Tape looked at him.

“What?”

Scissors became solemn.

“It's time.”

Yarn heard him.

“Time for what?”

Nobody answered.

Leola lifted Scissors.

He became still in her hand.

Yarn looked at Needle.

“Needle?”

He understood.

The scarf was finished.

And Yarn was still connected to the skein.

“No.”

Her voice trembled.

“Needle?”

He moved toward her.

“It's all right.”

“What he about to do?”

“Yarn—”

“Needle, what he about to do?”

Scissors opened.

Yarn saw the blades.

For the first time, Scissors wasn't funny.

Leola positioned him against the strand connecting Yarn to the finished scarf.

Yarn panicked.

“No.”

Needle spoke quickly.

“Listen to me.”

“NO.”

“Yarn.”

“Don't let him cut me!”

Needle couldn't move.

Leola held him on the table.

Scissors couldn't speak.

None of them could.

The blades closed.

*Snip.*

And Yarn screamed.

---

Silence.

She waited for pain.

For darkness.

For herself to disappear.

Nothing happened.

Yarn opened her eyes.

She was still there.

Smaller.

But there.

And across the table—

the scarf remained.

Part of her.

Separate from her.

But still her.

Leola's fingers tied off the final strand.

Needle looked between Yarn and the scarf.

“Yarn?”

She didn't answer.

“Yarn.”

Her voice shook.

“I'm here.”

Needle's whole body relaxed.

“You're here.”

Yarn looked at the scarf.

“So is she.”

Needle understood immediately.

The scarf wasn't *Yarn*.

And yet—

it was.

Something Yarn had become without ceasing to be herself.

Leola lifted the finished scarf.

Held it against the light.

And smiled.

Yarn saw the smile.

Her fear slowly gave way to something else.

Pride.

“Oh, my stars.”

Needle looked at the scarf.

The crooked first stitches were still there.

So were yesterday's better ones.

And today's.

Their arguments.

Their laughter.

Their mistake.

The three stitches they'd undone and remade.

All of it had become part of one thing.

“We made that,” Yarn whispered.

Needle looked at her.

“We did.”

---

That night, the basket was unusually quiet.

Scissors sat beside Yarn.

He hadn't made a joke since the cut.

Finally Yarn nudged him.

“You all right?”

Scissors looked surprised.

“Me?”

“You looked scared too.”

Scissors closed his blades.

“Cutting's my job.”

“I know.”

“Don't mean I like every cut.”

Yarn thought about that.

“Did it hurt?”

“Did it?”

Yarn considered.

“I thought it would.”

Scissors nodded.

“Sometimes the fear of a thing gets there before the thing itself.”

Old Spool murmured:

“And sometimes a necessary ending is how something finished gets to begin.”

Marker frowned.

“I don't understand none of that.”

Measuring Tape patted him.

“You ain't supposed to understand everything the first time.”

Marker considered this.

“Can I understand it tomorrow?”

“Maybe.”

“Tuesday?”

“Go to sleep.”

---

Yarn looked toward the chair.

The scarf was folded neatly across it.

“Who you think it's for?”

Hook shrugged.

“We'll find out.”

Yarn looked at Needle.

“You think they'll like it?”

“I do.”

“You know what part's my favorite?”

“The section where the tension is consistent?”

Yarn stared.

“Boy, if you don't—”

The basket laughed.

Needle smiled.

“Which part?”

Yarn pointed toward the beginning.

“That crooked stitch.”

Needle was surprised.

“The one you hated?”

“Mmm-hmm.”

“Why?”

“'Cause now I can see everything after it.”

Needle looked.

She was right.

The imperfect beginning made the improvement visible.

Yarn settled beside him.

“Needle?”

“Yes?”

“Thank you.”

“For what?”

“For knowin' me anywhere.”

Needle became quiet.

Yarn leaned gently against him.

This time he didn't complain about personal space.

Across the basket, Measuring Tape noticed.

She nudged Scissors.

Scissors opened one eye.

Saw them.

For once—

he didn't say a word.

He simply smiled and closed his eye again.

---

The next morning, Leola entered carrying the scarf.

Everybody froze.

She had wrapped it carefully in tissue paper.

Yarn watched from the basket.

“It's leavin'.”

Needle moved beside her.

“Yes.”

The scarf disappeared into a box.

Yarn felt unexpectedly sad.

Then Leola carried the box away.

Hours passed.

Nobody knew where it had gone.

Until that evening.

The front door opened.

Voices.

Leola's.

And another.

Then—

laughter.

Warm, surprised laughter.

Yarn rolled to the edge of the basket.

“What's happenin'?”

Marker climbed the rim.

“I can't see!”

“Move!”

“You move!”

Measuring Tape stretched upward.

Scissors tried to look over her.

Needle found a narrow gap.

In the next room stood a woman.

Around her neck—

sunset orange.

The scarf.

Yarn went completely still.

The woman touched it.

Held it against her cheek.

Then wrapped both ends around herself and hugged Leola.

Yarn couldn't hear every word.

She didn't need to.

She saw Leola's smile.

Saw the woman's eyes.

Saw the scarf resting around somebody's shoulders exactly where it was meant to be.

Yarn whispered:

“She loves it.”

Needle stood beside her.

“Yes.”

“We did that?”

“Yes.”

Yarn watched the woman touch the scarf again.

Something inside her changed.

Yesterday, she'd been afraid that becoming something meant losing herself.

Now she understood.

A piece of her had left the basket—

and carried warmth with it.

She leaned toward Needle.

“We made somebody happy.”

Needle looked at her rather than the scarf.

“We did.”

Yarn smiled.

A tear she didn't understand slipped into her fibers.

Needle noticed.

He said nothing.

He simply moved closer.

And this time, when their sides touched—

neither moved away.

---

That night, Yarn couldn't sleep.

Not because she was frightened.

Because the world had gotten bigger again.

First the yarn shop had been the whole world.

Then Leola's house.

Then the basket.

Then Needle.

Now she understood there were people beyond the house.

People she'd never meet.

People who could receive something made in this little room and feel loved by it.

Yarn looked toward Needle sleeping beside her.

Maybe making wasn't only about becoming something beautiful.

Maybe it was about **giving something of yourself away and discovering that somehow you weren't smaller for it.**

Somehow—

you became more.

Yarn closed her eyes.

Tomorrow there would be another project.

Another mistake.

Another lesson.

Another chance to discover what her hands—

no.

What **their** hands—

could make.

Outside, the last rainwater slipped from the roof.

Inside, Leola's basket rested quietly beside her chair.

And somewhere else in Milwaukee, a woman went to sleep with a sunset-orange scarf folded nearby—

never knowing how much laughter, fear, patience, trust and something that was beginning to look an awful lot like love had been stitched into every row.

**Thread by thread.**

**Stitch by stitch.**

**Together.**


### — End of Chapter Three —
